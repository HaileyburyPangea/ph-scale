# Expanded material catalogue

The `expanded-materials` branch contains 84 choices in six labelled groups, including
all 12 original materials. Both Macro and Micro use the same catalogue. The browser's
native dropdown supplies scrolling, group headings, keyboard navigation and type-ahead.

## How to interpret the examples

These are representative educational stock pH values, not specifications for real
products. Household products and natural samples vary with formulation, concentration,
temperature and age. Foods such as yoghurt, mustard and egg white represent a
water-containing sample or suspension; honey and toothpaste are explicitly in water.
Pure oil, pure alcohol and dry powders have not been assigned an aqueous pH.

The existing PhET dilution model is retained. It dilutes the initial excess hydrogen
or hydroxide ions; it does not calculate acid/base dissociation equilibria, buffer
capacity, chemical reactions, indicators, viscosity, or nonideal solution activities.
In particular, weak acids, buffered foods and biological fluids do not follow their
real dilution curves. Stock values for lab solutions assume approximately 25 °C;
strong-acid/base entries use the ideal dilute-solution approximation. M means mol/L.
Colours are illustrative and fade with dilution; red cabbage is not a working indicator.
Imaginary samples have invented colours and pH values and are labelled fictional.

This is a virtual exploration, not a recipe for mixing real household chemicals.

chosen within typical ranges or as illustrative formulations, not measured samples.
The original 12 stock values remain those supplied by PhET.
